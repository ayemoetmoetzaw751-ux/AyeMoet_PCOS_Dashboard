My Story

A Resilient & Patient Journey of fighting PCOS (Polycystic Ovary Syndrome).

Why This Journey is Meaningful?:
Living with PCOS is not an easy journey to overcome. I experienced extreme weight gains, body inflammation, whole month menstruation cycle, hair loss, slow body metabolism and side effects of PCOS. I managed to overcome through Fitness and limiting on my nutrition intake. I cried and had many challenges to be consistent in my workout, to be able to manage the nutrition under academic stress. This is not just my PCOS journey, but a journey of finding true physical and spiritual self.
2021
Start of Unhealthy Time

After Covid-19 and Unstable Political situations in Myanmar, my lifestyle and health became messy. I started gaining weight and having difficulties in my daily life, like finding it difficult to breathe while sleeping.

2022
Irregular Menstrual Cycle

The time I was familiar with CrossFit and Functional workouts. The irregular menstrual cycle started happening at this time.

2023
Active Management

Irregularities within my cycle persisted off and on. However, I remained tightly integrated with dedicated workout and fitness systems. So, I effectively managed and counter-balanced the visible side effects.

2024
Lifestyle Breakdown

A massive life disruption. I stopped exercising completely following a move to Yangon. Swallowed by a chaotic lifestyle, significant stress, and rapid weight fluctuations, my health indicators hit an absolute crisis point.

2025
Suffering PCOS

The year of intense physical and mental struggles. Facing highly extreme and prolonged menstrual occurrences, body inflammation, and mental breakdowns.

2026
Fight with My Immune System

Received PCOS diagnosis with a Nutritionist Doctor. Maintaining nutrition intake. Consistent in CrossFit workouts. Persistent stubbornness of weight, but showing good body composition.

What did I Learn?

What the collective metrics reveal when filtered with data accuracy and real honesty.

🏋️‍♀️

1. Exercise is the Primary Tool to Manage PCOS

Across the 5-year dataset, exercise stands as the single most critical lever for managing symptoms. During the 2022–2023 active functional training phases, regular physical activity kept self-reported PCOS severity scores at a manageable baseline average of 3.0 out of 10. When exercise dropped to zero in 2024 following the move to Yangon, severity metrics immediately climbed to an all-time peak of 10.0 out of 10, proving the direct protective value of functional movement.

🩸

2. Tracking the Menstrual Cycle Tells the True Health Story

A consistent cycle serves as the ultimate indicator of foundational physical recovery. The data shows that during my 2024 lifestyle breakdown, standard tracking sequences completely disappeared, replaced by an uninterrupted 12-month string of irregular or skipped markers. Reclaiming a predictable rhythm during the 2026 recovery phase is the clearest indicator that hormones have stabilized, showing that cycle health is a far more useful metric than any number on a bathroom scale.

⚖️

3. Weight and PCOS Control Work Independently

The metrics clearly show that body mass index and internal health function work independently of each other. While body weight has stayed on a stubborn plateau between 178 lbs and 184 lbs across 2025 and 2026, my internal health markers tell a very different story. During this exact same timeframe, my subjective mood architecture stabilized from a low score of 2.0 up to an active 8.0 out of 10, while overall PCOS symptom severity trended down from 10.0 to 4.0.

Future Plan

Data-backed strategic course corrections aimed at maintaining sustainable health and long-term vitality.

⚡

1. Consistent Workout

Maintain consistency in workouts: The data confirms that breaking my physical fitness schedule causes immediate health risks. When training dropped to 0% continuity in 2024, my PCOS severity rating spiked by 70% (from 3.0 up to 10.0). Therefore, I will lock in a non-negotiable schedule of at least 4 functional training sessions per week, treating exercise as essential medical self-care to ensure my severity indexes never cross the baseline threshold of 5.0 again.

🥗

2. Focus on Menstrual Cycle And Nutrition Intake

Shift focus away from weight outcomes: Because the tracking records show a distinct 24-month process where weight fluctuated by less than 4% despite major changes in diet and activity, I will stop using weight as my primary metric for health. Instead, I will evaluate success based on cycle regularity and maintaining nutrition intake, supporting my metabolic health without triggering the cortisol spikes caused by scale-related anxiety.

🌙

3. Maintain Sleep Routine

Protect sleep windows to stabilize mood: The sleep-to-mood scatter plot displays a clear threshold effect: when sleep durations fell below 6.0 hours, reported mood indicators consistently dropped below a score of 4.0. To actively manage my psychological resilience, I will protect a strict 7-5 hours nightly sleep window, using rest as a primary defense against stress-induced hormonal flare-ups.

Ethics & Responsibility

Critical framework assessing data boundaries, biological tracking limitations, and responsible representation of self-reported metrics.

🔒

Privacy Statement

What data is included? This asset captures monthly measurements spanning January 2021 through December 2026, comprising physical body weight values (lbs), calculated sleep durations, self-reported mood states, physical fitness tiers, cycle regularity labels, and synthesized PCOS symptom intensity indices.

What is anonymized? To minimize digital privacy risks while maintaining structural openness, direct identifiers like personal healthcare registry numbers, clinical medical facility names, and specific medication dosages have been entirely excluded from the repository. Data processing strips all local timestamp values, exposing only generic month/year buckets to ensure absolute privacy while maintaining analysis value.

⚠️

Bias & Limitation Disclosure

Memory Bias: Historical metrics collected throughout 2021 and 2022 rely on retrospective tracking logs and memory recall, introducing possible variation where specific baseline sleep habits or mild cycle shifts could be under-reported or smoothed over.

Small Dataset Constraint: The primary scope contains exactly 61 rows representing an individual tracking ledger. It cannot be used to generalize algorithmic trends or construct generalized endocrine models for broader clinical groups.

Subjective Scoring Frameworks: Elements such as the 1–10 Mood Score and PCOS Severity Scale are self-assessed by personal observation. These parameters reflect qualitative perceptions and are subject to personal bias, rather than objective, lab-tested biochemical metrics.

📊

Visualization Justification

Chart Choices: Stacked bar components were selected for the Menstrual Status distribution to convey tracking completeness and display cyclical ratios clearly. The Sleep vs. Mood correlation chart leverages an unlinked scatter layout to highlight raw clusters without inventing linear trends where the data is spread out.

Misinterpretation Risk Mitigation: Line elements representing long-term weight indicators present a distinct risk of causing anxiety over minor weight plateaus. To prevent false conclusions, a direct PCOS Intensity Trend is plotted directly alongside it. This structure visually emphasizes that clinical progress exists even when body weight metrics remain temporarily fixed.

🎯

Responsible Decision-Making & Limitations

Framework Limitations: The choices detailed in the execution map (uncompromising CrossFit routines, calorie tracking, and sleep defenses) are optimization steps built on this individual history. They operate purely as wellness updates and do not replace professional medical advice, target clinical medication paths, or substitute for direct laboratory blood testing.
